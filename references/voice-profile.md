# Reference voice: methodological explanation

## Status and use

This provisional profile reflects an initial reading of selected introductions,
study-purpose passages, methods, results, and discussions in papers coauthored by
Timothy R. Konold and Elizabeth A. Sanders. It describes useful editorial choices,
not a measured stylistic model or an attribution of individual authorship. The
user has approved the general direction; passage-level preferences remain open
to refinement through edits of the user's own writing.

Apply the profile to organization and explanation. Preserve the manuscript's
scientific content and the user's explicit preferences. Write original prose;
do not reproduce distinctive sentences, rhetorical signatures, or an article's
topic-specific recommendations. The profile works without access to Zotero.

## Section-specific choices

| Section | Preferred editorial direction |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction | Define the methodological problem, locate the relevant prior evidence, explain what it leaves unresolved, and connect that issue to the study question. Give readers a concrete consequence when supported. |
| Theory or background | Introduce quantities before using them. Connect equations and assumptions to verbal explanations of the mechanism. Use a focused example when it helps readers distinguish cases. |
| Study purpose | State the question and the conditions or contrasts needed to answer it. Connect each important design choice to that question. A short roadmap can help. |
| Methods | Separate the generating process, fitted models, manipulated conditions, and evaluation criteria. Explain consequential choices while retaining enough detail to reconstruct the design. |
| Results | Lead with the comparison or pattern relevant to the question, then give the supplied quantitative support and exceptions. Keep interactions and conditioning visible. |
| Discussion | Connect findings back to the mechanism and prior work; distinguish what the results imply for an analyst under different circumstances. Bound recommendations by the design and limitations. |

These are functions to assess, not a mandatory sequence of headings or a template
every paragraph must follow.

## Sentence and paragraph preferences

- Preserve explanatory depth. Pair a technical distinction with why it matters.
- Let a paragraph develop a coherent claim through explanation, evidence, and
  implication as needed; avoid requiring every paragraph to contain all three.
- Use explicit logical connections and brief roadmaps where the relationship
  would otherwise be hard to follow. Keep recognizable study framing when useful.
- Use stable names for quantities and models, conventional statistical language,
  and precise qualifications. Prefer direct comparisons to vague evaluations.
- Retain useful contextual detail and citations; tighten repeated motivation,
  redundant study summaries, and parenthetical material that obscures the point.
- Judge sentence length and punctuation by readability. The reference papers'
  long sentences, intensifiers, and repetitions are not automatic preferences.

When the user provides a draft, demonstrate the profile on a manageable passage
and explain consequential organizational decisions. Record recurring preferences
only after they are supported by the user's feedback; a one-off exception need
not become a standing rule. User-authored samples can refine or supersede this
coauthored reference profile.

If further calibration is requested, compare sections with the same function and
check passages not used to formulate the profile. Describe this as qualitative
editorial assessment; do not report an unmeasured voice-match score. Retrieve
specific reference passages only when needed and available. Do not make an entire
