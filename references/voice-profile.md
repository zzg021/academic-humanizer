# Reference voice: a clear methodological story

## Editorial direction

The useful model in these papers is how they make a methodological problem
intelligible. Readers can follow the practice or assumption at issue, the source
of the difficulty, the response, and the consequences. Technical development
advances that account.

The user's preference is to preserve this clear research story and avoid
defensive writing that buries the contribution beneath qualifications and
implementation details.

## Passages for an explicit reference-voice revision

Choose passages that serve the same explanatory purpose as the text being
revised. A conceptual introduction, an equation-led explanation, and a results
paragraph ask different things of their readers. Read the relevant prose with
its equation or figure; there is no need to read every passage below for each edit.

These readings illustrate how a line of reasoning develops through technical
material. They do not prescribe sentence lengths, identical paragraph patterns,
or the same amount of detail for every manuscript. Use the comparison to decide
what readers need to understand next, where notation helps, and which details
deserve emphasis.

## Apply this to a manuscript

- **Introduction:** Establish the research problem and its source. Use prior work
  to develop that reasoning, and make the paper's response understandable.
- **Technical development:** Explain the mechanism and the idea behind the
  response. Use equations, examples, and definitions where they advance it.
- **Methods:** Connect the design to the question. Place implementation details
  where readers can use them without losing the rationale.
- **Results:** Organize around the findings that answer the question. Explain
  patterns and revealing contrasts; use tables or supporting passages for
  exhaustive condition summaries.
- **Discussion:** Explain what the work resolves and how it changes understanding
  or practice. Give consequential boundaries their place without repeatedly
  retreating from the main finding.

Use direct statements, stable terminology, and connected paragraphs. Preserve
explanatory depth while cutting repetitive framing and defensive preambles.
A useful qualification sharpens the argument. Its mere presence does not make
a passage more rigorous.
