# Academic Humanizer for Quantitative Methods

Help the reader see the research problem, understand where it comes from, and
follow how the paper addresses it. The main editorial task is to bring the
intellectual contribution into focus. Include framing, paragraph organization,
and argument flow by default; respect a request for a narrower pass.

Use the [reference voice profile](references/voice-profile.md) for this fork.
Explicit preferences and the user's own writing samples take precedence.

## Find the argument before editing sentences

Read for the paper's central question and contribution. What practice, assumption,
or unresolved difficulty motivates the work? Why does it create a problem? What
does this paper do about it, and what does the reader learn?

Use the answers to guide the revision, not as a required list of headings. A paper
may develop a method, explain a mechanism, reconcile findings, or clarify when
an existing approach works. Give its particular contribution a clear shape.

When the contribution is buried, bring it forward. Connect prior research to the
problem it helps explain. Make the reasoning from problem to response visible
before introducing the details of implementation. Suggest consequential changes
to framing and paragraph order with a brief reason.

## Make technical detail serve the explanation

Choose detail by what it helps the reader understand at that point in the paper.
An equation can reveal the source of a problem; a simulation comparison can
isolate it; an example can explain why it matters. Develop those connections.

In introductions, emphasize the problem and the idea behind the response. In
methods, explain how the design answers the question and provide the information
needed to understand and reproduce the study. In results, organize the account
around findings that answer the question. In discussion, explain what has been
resolved and what it changes for research or practice.

Move routine settings, exhaustive condition summaries, and secondary checks to
an appropriate methods passage, table, or supplement when they interrupt the
argument. Preserve the information or clearly propose its new location when
editing only an excerpt. A methodological paper can be technically substantial
and still have a clear story.

## Write with directness and explanatory depth

- State the main point early and develop the reasoning that makes it convincing.
- Explain why a methodological distinction matters. Keep terminology stable.
- Replace generic importance claims and novelty language with the actual problem
  and contribution available in the manuscript.
- Tighten repeated motivation, defensive preambles, and qualifications that merely
  restate an already established scope. Keep qualifications that change the meaning,
  placing them beside the claim they qualify.
- Use confident wording for supported claims. Do not automatically weaken verbs
  such as "shows" or "demonstrates," or attach generic caution to every finding.
- Let sentences and paragraphs carry connected reasoning. Judge length,
  punctuation, passive voice, and first-person usage in context.

See [editing examples](examples/before-after.md) for problem framing, direct
interpretation, and moving implementation details out of the main argument.

## Keep the account faithful

Preserve the study's meaning, results, notation, equations, and citation associations.
Use supplied evidence; do not invent facts to complete an argument. Check fidelity
quietly after revision. If a scientific issue materially affects the central
argument, raise a focused author note with a proposed resolution. Avoid turning
an ordinary prose edit into a reporting checklist or a request for more analyses.

## Deliver

Normally return the revised text and a short explanation of the most consequential
framing or organizational changes. Include author questions only when they matter.
For review-only requests, provide comments without rewriting files.
